## 1. Summary

**Is README §1.1 right that the 12 modules are built "exactly as written"? Not quite.** Functionally, all 12 modules are covered and most of them are extended. The six claim-stage names match the Arabic letter for letter, and so do the surplus formula's four terms, the three investment contracts, the 30-day renewal alert, the hourly refresh, ">20 KPIs" and ">10 dashboards". The failures are in **technology, schedule, contract scope and a few softened mechanisms**, not in module concepts:

1. **The technology stack deviates and nothing records the client's acceptance.** The brief names Python with Odoo or ERPNext (backend), React, PostgreSQL, RESTful APIs, Jinja2, Celery, Metabase or Power BI, and AWS, Azure or private cloud. Its §6 then commits to "the open-source licences and systems named in the project scope". The README replaces Python, Odoo/ERPNext, Jinja2 and Celery with TypeScript, Next.js, headless Chromium and pg-boss on Vercel and Neon. D8 says "the client knows and accepts this", but no signed document supports that. §19 does not trace the backend clause at all, and D00-17 (the signed-deviation list) leaves the stack out.
2. **The schedule is contractually inconsistent.** The brief says "12 weeks in weekly sprints" and "90 days total implementation". The README keeps only the 90 days and plans 13 sprints. Plan V1 shows UAT signed on day 97 at P50 and go-live on day 161 at P50. It recommends restating the contract, but no work item obtains that restatement.
3. **The go-live critical path runs through items the brief does not contain.** These are the full ARIMA migration and parallel run, the i-text review (H23), the legal opinion on hosting in Frankfurt (which exists only because of our hosting choice), the masked-sample reconciliation for the P1 exit, and the payroll control sheet at G-P2. Brief §6 puts anything outside the 12 modules out of scope. README §16 rewrites that clause as "beyond this README".
4. **Three mechanisms are softened:** Metabase/Power BI becomes a connection guide while the dashboards are built in ECharts; the payment gateways are not live or instant at go-live; and the digital-identity link falls back to manual checks.
